# Yummy-ness, Masarap-ness, Lekker-ness: A Long-Form Report on the "Food Vibe" Trilingual Taxonomy and the Economics of Turning It Into a Data Asset

## Summary

This report analyzes the linguistic, cultural, psychological, sociological, and economic aspects of the "Food Vibe" family's trilingual food-rating taxonomy using the playful "-ness" suffix on "yummy," "masarap," and "lekker." It highlights the rich cultural meanings of these words, the social bonding role of food photography and rating in diaspora families, and the challenges of monetizing such data as a commercial asset under GDPR and market realities.

## Key Points

- "Lekker" (Dutch) and "masarap" (Tagalog) are culturally rich, sensory-pleasure words extending beyond food to bodily and social wellbeing.
- The "-ness" suffix mock-quantifies these subjective pleasures into a playful hedonic scale.
- Food photography and sharing in family group chats serve as digital commensality, strengthening bonds across distance.
- The economics of selling such richly labeled family food data are challenging; market prices for similar data are far below the €12 per datapoint honorarium proposed.
- GDPR and copyright considerations mean commercializing family food photos requires careful consent, rights clearance, and data governance.
- The best near-term model is a data cooperative or foundation funded by grants or patrons, treating payments as honoraria rather than market prices.
- A boutique, well-documented, consented dataset with cross-cultural affective labels could attract research partnerships but is unlikely to be a high-volume commercial asset.

## Recommendations

1. Keep the activity private and household-based to avoid GDPR scope.
2. If commercializing, secure written IP licenses and GDPR consent, and adopt a "food only, no faces" capture rule.
3. Standardize rating schema and metadata for analyzability.
4. Structure governance as a Dutch stichting or cooperative with transparent, revocable consent.
5. Pursue research partnerships or grants to fund honoraria rather than rely on direct data sales.
6. Price realistically if selling: expect low-thousands one-time fees for boutique datasets, not €12 per datapoint.
